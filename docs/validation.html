Euler vs RANS Comparison

The inviscid Euler simulation neglects boundary layer effects. The RANS SST k-omega simulation includes viscous effects, producing a lower exit Mach number due to boundary layer displacement. The following comparison covers all four engine configurations.

Engine Euler Mach RANS Mach Difference Isentropic Mach Euler Error
Merlin 1D 4.4717 4.2547 4.85% 4.4593 0.28%
Raptor SL 5.1431 4.8180 6.32% 5.3933 4.64%
RS-25 6.4055 5.2510 18.03% 6.5463 2.15%
RL10B-2 7.4913 -- -- 8.7362 14.25%

SpaceX Merlin 1D

Euler (Inviscid)

RANS SST (Viscous)

The Merlin 1D shows excellent agreement between Euler and isentropic theory (0.28% error). The RANS boundary layer reduces exit Mach by 4.85%, consistent with the moderate expansion ratio (16:1) and compact nozzle length (0.74m).

SpaceX Raptor SL

Euler (Inviscid)

RANS SST (Viscous)

The Raptor SL's higher chamber pressure (33 MPa) produces a slightly larger Euler-isentropic discrepancy (4.64%). The RANS viscous correction (6.32%) is larger than Merlin's due to the higher Reynolds number and longer nozzle (1.19m).

RS-25 (Space Shuttle / SLS)

Euler (Inviscid)

RANS SST (Viscous)

The RS-25 shows a significant RANS correction (18.03%) due to its long diverging section (3.17m) and high expansion ratio (77.5:1). The extended nozzle length amplifies viscous losses throughout the flow field.

RL10B-2 (Delta IV Upper Stage)

Euler (Inviscid)

RANS SST (Viscous)

The RL10B-2's extreme expansion ratio (285:1) and nozzle length (3.65m) produce the largest Euler-isentropic error (14.25%). The current mesh resolution may be insufficient to fully resolve the extreme expansion, indicating opportunities for mesh refinement studies.

  • Boundary layer effect scales with nozzle length: Longer nozzles (RS-25, RL10B-2) show larger Euler-RANS differences
  • High chamber pressure increases viscous effects: Raptor SL at 33 MPa shows larger RANS correction than Merlin at 9.7 MPa
  • Expansion ratio affects accuracy: Extreme ratios (285:1) challenge mesh resolution requirements
  • Physical correctness: RANS results consistently show lower exit Mach than Euler, matching expected viscous behavior
